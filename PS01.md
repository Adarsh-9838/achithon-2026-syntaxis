## Problem Statement

Modern applications frequently request permissions that enable access to sensitive information and device capabilities, including contacts, location, the camera, the microphone, and stored files. However, ordinary users often struggle to understand what these technical declarations mean, what privacy implications they may have, and whether the requested access is consistent with the application's stated functionality.

Privacy policies add another layer of complexity because they often describe data collection, processing, sharing, and retention practices in lengthy legal or technical language. Manually comparing these policies with an application's declared permissions can be difficult, time-consuming, and prone to misinterpretation.

The proposed project aims to develop an Explainable Application Privacy and Permission Analyzer that accepts permission lists, readable AndroidManifest.xml files, and optional privacy-policy text. The system will extract permission declarations, map known permissions to a curated knowledge base, and translate their technical meaning into clear, understandable explanations.

Each finding will include the exact permission identifier, its potential privacy implications, and a reference to the relevant input declaration. When a privacy policy is provided, the tool will identify relevant excerpts and generate concise summaries supported by the original text. Unsupported or unknown permissions will be explicitly flagged rather than assigned speculative explanations.

The system will distinguish between declared application capabilities, statements contained in a supplied privacy policy, and evidence of actual application behavior. An optional analysis module will compare declared permissions with the application's stated purpose and highlight potentially unexpected requests as questions for further investigation, without automatically classifying the application as malicious.

## Key Deliverable

A functional web prototype that accepts permission lists or readable AndroidManifest.xml files and generates a downloadable, evidence-linked privacy analysis report.

The report will contain extracted permissions, plain-language explanations, potential privacy implications, supporting input references, unknown or unsupported declarations, and summaries of relevant privacy-policy excerpts when provided.

An optional extension will compare permissions against the application's stated functionality and raise review questions when a permission appears unexpected, without treating the mismatch alone as proof of malicious behavior.

### Expected Outcome

The project will demonstrate how structured permission analysis, curated security knowledge, evidence-grounded language processing, and explainable reporting can make application privacy information more accessible to nontechnical users.

Its effectiveness will be evaluated through permission extraction accuracy, explanation correctness, source traceability, policy-summary grounding, unknown-permission detection, and the rate of unsupported or misleading findings.
